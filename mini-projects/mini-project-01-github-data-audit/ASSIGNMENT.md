# Decision Lab 1: GitHub Data Audit and Decision Framing

## Purpose

Dr. K's Late-Night Analytics Café has plenty of orders, several locations, ambitious plans, and a spreadsheet that has clearly been through some things.
Before management builds dashboards, predicts wait times, or optimizes staffing, one question comes first: Can the data be trusted enough to support a decision?
Your team will act as the café's analytics consulting group. You will inspect the structure of the order data, identify and document quality problems, build a defensible cleaned version, and explain what management may - and may not - conclude from it. This is not a hunt for random typos. It is a decision-readiness audit.
*The Dr. K rule*: A spreadsheet can look very confident while being completely wrong. Your job is to make the evidence earn our confidence.

### Final Report — 75%

**Due:** Check Harvey.**
*Deliverable 2: Final Written Report*
Submit one 5-7 page PDF report, excluding the title page and AI-use appendix. Write for Dr. K as the decision maker: concise, evidence-based, and understandable without opening the Excel workbook.
•	Executive summary: state the decision question, readiness judgment, principal finding, and recommendation.
•	Data and audit: explain the dataset, important data types, most consequential quality problems, cleaning rules, and reconciliation.
•	Analysis: present the management question, method, key result, and at least one readable visual copied from the Excel workbook.
•	Decision: provide the recommendation, limitations, prevention controls, and next measurement; include the AI-use log as an appendix.

*Deliverable 3: Team Recording (Optional)*
•	Submit a focused 5-8 minute recording. Every team member must speak and explain a substantive part of the work.
•	Explain the decision question and the two quality problems that created the greatest decision risk.
•	Explain the most important cleaning rules and how the team verified them.
•	Interpret the PivotTable and chart; explain what the evidence shows and what it does not prove.
•	State the readiness judgment and recommendation. Submit a tested file or accessible link and include accurate captions or a transcript.

## Final advice

If the dataset looks perfectly clean after five minutes, your team is probably not done looking.

AI may suggest the cleaning plan, but it does not get to testify as an expert witness unless your team can reproduce the evidence.
